They work differently but both protect your brain

What the research really means

For years, we've been told cardio is king for brain health. Running for memory. Cycling for cognition. But new research reveals something surprising: lifting weights might be even better for your brain.

A 10-week resistance training program increased BDNF—the brain molecule linked to memory and learning—by 65.2%. Even more striking? Resistance training showed a stronger effect (0.76) than aerobic exercise (0.48) on this crucial brain-protective molecule.

Here's what's happening: Cardio increases gray matter in your cerebellum and temporal lobe. Resistance training boosts volume in your basal ganglia and strengthens cerebellar connections. Different pathways, complementary benefits.

The bottom line? Your brain doesn't care whether you're running or squatting—it just wants you moving in multiple ways. Stop treating weights like they're only for aesthetics. They're medicine for your mind.
