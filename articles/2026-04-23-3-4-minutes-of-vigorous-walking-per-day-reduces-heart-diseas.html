"You don't need a gym membership"

What counts

Vigorous lifestyle activity includes:

  • Power walking to catch a bus
  • Climbing stairs quickly
  • Carrying heavy shopping uphill
  • Any brief burst that makes you breathe hard

The key: short bursts throughout your day, not one long session

What the research really means

Think you need hours at the gym for a healthy heart? Think again.

New research from the British Journal of Sports Medicine tracked over 22,000 adults who didn't exercise regularly. They wore activity trackers 24/7 for a week, then researchers monitored their cardiovascular health for up to 9 years.

The results? Women who averaged just 3.4 minutes per day of vigorous lifestyle activity—power walking, stair climbing, carrying shopping—had a 45% lower risk of major heart problems and a 67% lower risk of heart failure.

Even better: just 1.2 minutes per day showed a 30% reduction in cardiovascular events.

This isn't about structured workouts. It's about making everyday moments count. Sprint to catch your bus. Take the stairs fast. Carry your groceries uphill. These brief bursts of intensity add up to serious heart protection.

The best exercise is the one you'll actually do—and apparently, you only need a few minutes.
