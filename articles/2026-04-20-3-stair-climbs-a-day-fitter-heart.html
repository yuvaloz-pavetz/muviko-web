Why stairs are the most underrated exercise machine

VO₂ max — your body's maximum oxygen uptake — is one of the strongest predictors of longevity. People with high VO₂ max live longer, get fewer chronic diseases, and perform better under stress. It used to be considered something only athletes could improve meaningfully.

This study changes that assumption. Researchers found that brief, repeated stair climbs — the kind you could do in any office building, apartment block, or parking garage — produced a 17.1% improvement in VO₂ max. That's a number most gym programmes would struggle to match.

The key mechanism: stair climbing is inherently high-intensity. Your heart rate spikes within seconds. Your muscles fire against gravity. Even a 90-second climb delivers a meaningful cardiovascular stimulus. Do it three times across the day, and you've hit the effective dose.

What the science is actually saying

This isn't about replacing your training. It's about what happens in the gaps — the moments between meetings, the commute, the lunch break. Most people have access to stairs. Almost no one uses them intentionally.

The study didn't require a specific staircase, a specific building, or any gear. It required effort. Go fast enough to feel your heart rate rise. That's the protocol.

Three times a day. Under two minutes each. The infrastructure is already around you.

What counts

Any stairs. Any building.

  • Office stairwell between meetings
  • Apartment stairs instead of the lift
  • A parking garage on your lunch break
  • Train station stairs — skip the escalator

Intensity matters more than duration — go fast enough to feel your heart rate rise. That's the dose.

The bigger picture

This research sits within a growing body of evidence on "exercise snacks" — brief, scattered bouts of movement that collectively deliver the benefits once thought to require dedicated gym sessions.

What makes stair climbing particularly compelling is the barrier-to-entry. You don't need to change clothes. You don't need to warm up. You don't need to block time in your calendar. You just need to choose the stairs — three times — with intent.

Most people walk past a staircase and press the button. They're leaving cardiovascular fitness on the table every single day.

The takeaway

Three short stair climbs a day — each under two minutes, done with effort — is enough to increase your cardiovascular fitness by 17%. VO₂ max is one of the best predictors of long-term health. The stairs in your building are one of the most effective tools for improving it.
